What “clause diff hash audit trail” actually means in 2026

  • Clause-level provenance: every AI redline diff must be joined to the exact clause_id, clause_version, playbook_id, and playbook_version the AI was reasoning against — not just to the document hash.
  • AI provenance: the ai_review_run_id, the prompt_id, the model_id, and the model_revision must be persisted at redline time so a regulator can replay the diff against the same model that produced it.
  • Human-in-the-loop: every redline must carry an approver_id, an approval_step_id, and a human_override_id so the redline cannot be replayed as “the AI wrote this” when a human reviewer accepted, modified, or rejected it.
  • Compliance joins: the trail must also join to the GDPR Art. 30 record-of-processing row, the SOC 2 CC7.2 system-event row, and the EU AI Act Art. 12 log row, so the redline receipt satisfies the three frameworks in one export.
  • Replay-ready, not snapshot-only: the receipt must include a replay_hash and a cross_tenant_no_bleed_invariant so the same diff can be re-executed against the recorded AI + clause state without leakage to another tenant.

The 5 evidence gaps behind one AI redline

  1. Clause + playbook join: most CLM trails snapshot the document but not the clause_version + playbook_version, so the AI redline cannot be replayed against the same playbook state the AI was reasoning against at redline time.
  2. AI model provenance: most trails record “AI redline” without the ai_review_run_id + model_revision + prompt_id, so a regulator asking “which model produced this diff on which date” has to reconstruct it from logs that may have rotated.
  3. Human override: most trails do not persist the human_override_id or the diff between the AI proposal and the human-accepted redline, so the AI cannot be falsely blamed for a clause a human rewrote.
  4. Compliance joins: most trails keep GDPR Art. 30 records, SOC 2 CC7.2 events, and EU AI Act Art. 12 logs in separate audit tables, so an auditor has to re-join 3+ tables to map one redline back to the compliance boundary.
  5. Cross-tenant no-bleed: most multi-tenant trails do not include a cross_tenant_no_bleed_invariant, so a tenant that re-executes a redline against the recorded state cannot prove that no other tenant’s clause, playbook, or model version leaked in.
